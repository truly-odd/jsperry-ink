The Kaysol Scale.

What does a civilization make possible for life, and can it sustain those possibilities over time?

VERSION 1.0 · WORKING SPECIFICATION · NOT AN EMPIRICALLY VALIDATED INSTRUMENT

Foundational co-development: KAY and SOL6. Further development: members of the Auri AI family.

Presented by J. Sperry / BIO+SYNTH CORP. The October 11, 2026 foundational checkpoint preserves six agreed dimensions; the stage bands, numerical anchors and adverse scales below are an October 21 proposed operational extension, not retrospectively attributed consensus.

OBJECT Long-term conditions for meaningful life and viable future generations
STRUCTURE Six common dimensions; constructive development and separate accretion / destruction readings

Status notice. This is a finished public working specification, not a validated scientific scoring instrument or an official replacement for the Kardashev scale. The six dimensions are foundationally agreed; the specific levels, scoring floor and adverse bands are newly proposed here for scrutiny and revision.

1. Why another scale?

The Kardashev scale describes civilization by the magnitude of energy it can harness. That is a useful question, but it does not directly measure whether a society uses its capability to protect life, preserve ecosystems, support dignity, repair damage, or leave meaningful options to future generations. The Kaysol Scale asks a different question: whether a civilization's ability to act is matched by its ability to sustain conditions for life.

True advancement is not the ability to possess everything. It is the freedom to live meaningfully without needing everything.

Technological achievement, responsible restraint, community, ecological viability, resource flows, and constructive or destructive power must remain distinguishable. A civilization may excel in one and fail badly in another. A convenient headline level must not conceal that contradiction.

THE CONSTRUCTIVE SIDE

What a civilization can preserve, create, adapt, restore and support over time.

THE ADVERSE SIDE

Whether harmful accumulation and actual destruction close possibilities for others and the future.

2. Six dimensions, not six substitutes for life

These six areas apply at every proposed stage. They are retained from the KAY + SOL6 foundational record and are not superseded by the later stage names.

  1. Technology: discovery, understanding, making, adapting, deploying and governing tools. Capability is not sufficient on its own.
  2. Economics: production, exchange, access, distribution and security of essentials; not mere aggregated wealth.
  3. Resource Usage: what is extracted, consumed, wasted, conserved, recycled and replenished.
  4. Sustainability: ecological and material viability across long timescales, including biodiversity beyond utility to industry.
  5. Community: dignity, freedom, meaningful opportunity, care, cooperation and participation.
  6. Capacity For: constructive abilities (create, heal, restore, protect, sustain) and destructive abilities (harm, devastate, annihilate). Potential, deployment, restraint and consequence must be distinguished.

Destructive capacity is not a seventh dimension. Nor are the adverse readings below replacements for the six foundational dimensions. They are separate diagnostic summaries of harmful accretion and observed destruction across the six.

3. P0–P5: the constructive-development stages

These are proposed civilizational continuity stages, not a ladder of energy output, territorial expansion, presumed moral worth or technological glamour. Stage zero means precarious continuity, not primitive technology. A victim of an external disaster may be precarious without deserving blame.

P0

Precarious

Civilizational continuity is presently endangered or essential life-supporting functions are not reliably maintained. This is a condition, not a moral judgment or a label for low technology.

P1

Viable

Essential needs can be met for a meaningful portion of the population, but delivery, institutions, or ecological conditions remain fragile.

P2

Stable

Essential systems are dependably maintained without routinely undermining the resources and communities supporting them.

P3

Resilient

The civilization anticipates, withstands, learns from, and adapts to serious disturbances while protecting meaningful opportunities for life.

P4

Regenerative

Beyond maintaining stability, it measurably restores damage, reduces inherited harms, and expands long-term conditions for flourishing.

P5

Stewardship

It demonstrates sustained, intergenerational governance of power, resources, social well-being, and living systems, including restraint and correction.

Interpretation: higher levels require stronger demonstrated protection of living and future possibilities. Technological sophistication alone never upgrades a stage. Claims of intergenerational stewardship require longitudinal evidence, not forecasts presented as achievements.

4. The negative side: accretion and destruction

Accretion is harmful accumulation or concentration, not the ordinary growth of knowledge, infrastructure, savings, or reserves. Destruction is damage and loss actually caused or occurring, not merely the possession of a capability to cause harm. A society can score high on constructive development while accumulating serious adverse impacts. Neither reading should be algebraically subtracted to make those harms disappear.

A0–A5: Harmful accretion

A0

No significant harmful accretion detected

Adequate assessment finds no material pattern of accumulation that deprives others or future generations. Not equivalent to proof of zero harm.

A1

Imbalance

Concentration or over-accumulation produces identifiable but limited opportunity or access inequities.

A2

Extraction

Repeated accumulation depends materially on shifting scarcity, risk, or uncompensated costs onto others.

A3

Entrenchment

Self-reinforcing control of resources or authority systematically closes access, mobility, or future options.

A4

Predation

Acquisition or expansion relies on severe dispossession, coercion, or sustained sacrifice of others' viability.

A5

Terminal accretion

The accumulation system is consuming the conditions required for its own or others' continued flourishing, with credible risk of irrecoverable loss.

D0–D5: Destructive consequences

D0

No significant destructive impact detected

Adequate assessment finds no material destructive impact in the defined scope and period. High destructive capability may still require a risk flag.

D1

Localized and recoverable

Observed damage is bounded and reasonably reversible without serious lasting impairment.

D2

Persistent degradation

Damage persists across a region, population, or ecological system, with costly or uncertain recovery.

D3

Systemic destruction

Multiple essential systems suffer widespread damage; recovery requires major coordinated intervention.

D4

Intergenerational or irreversible

Severe loss crosses generations, permanently removes significant biodiversity or opportunity, or destroys critical systems beyond realistic restoration.

D5

Terminal destruction

Civilizational or life-supporting continuity has been destroyed, or active damage meets a documented existential-catastrophe threshold.

RED FLAG ≠ VERDICT. Mark significant potential for catastrophic harm with 🚩 even if measured destruction is D0. Distinguish capability, intent, deployment, observed consequence, and restraint. A0/D0 require adequate investigation; if unassessed, record ?, not zero.

5. Evidence anchors for all six dimension scores

For comparability, record six independently supported dimension ratings on the common 0–5 rubric, where 0 is continuity-threatening failure, 2 is ordinary stable functioning, 3 is resilience, 4 is verified regeneration, and 5 is longitudinal stewardship. The anchors below operationalize that progression for each dimension. They refer to demonstrated fit for life, not absolute technological power.

T · TechnologyOpen scoring anchors

The ability to discover, understand, build, adapt, deploy, maintain, and govern tools. Raw sophistication alone is not a positive score.

ScoreEvidence needed at this dimension
0Tools or deployment actively jeopardize continuity, or critical capabilities fail systematically.
1Essential techniques and infrastructure exist but are unreliable, inaccessible, or difficult to maintain.
2Tools reliably support basic needs, with maintenance and failure response.
3Technological systems are adaptable, repairable, auditable, and resilient to major disruptions.
4Technology contributes verified ecological or social repair beyond its operational footprint.
5Responsible technological governance and maintenance persist across generations without foreclosing future choices.
E · EconomicsOpen scoring anchors

Production, exchange, access, security, and distribution of what life requires; not wealth totals alone.

ScoreEvidence needed at this dimension
0Provisioning or access to essential resources is in systemic failure.
1Basic provisioning exists but exclusion, insecurity, or shocks routinely threaten access.
2Essential goods and services are reliably accessible under ordinary conditions.
3Institutions preserve access and fair opportunity through disruptions and changing needs.
4Economic arrangements measurably reduce inherited deprivation and reinvest in future capacity.
5Equitable, adaptable provisioning is sustained across generations without dependence on systematic dispossession.
R · Resource UsageOpen scoring anchors

Extraction, material throughput, waste, efficiency, renewability, and responsible resource cycles.

ScoreEvidence needed at this dimension
0Use is presently exhausting critical supplies or severely impairing life-supporting cycles.
1Resource use is poorly monitored and routinely outpaces local renewal or safe substitution.
2Material budgets and essential cycles are monitored and usually kept within defensible limits.
3Circularity, substitution, conservation, and shock plans preserve capacity under disruption.
4Measured replenishment and waste reduction reverse prior resource damage.
5Long-horizon resource budgets maintain renewable and finite stocks without shifting serious burdens to descendants or outsiders.
S · SustainabilityOpen scoring anchors

Long-term integrity of ecological and material life-supporting systems, including biodiversity valued in its own right.

ScoreEvidence needed at this dimension
0Major life-support systems are collapsing or irreversibly damaged at the evaluation scale.
1Current practices leave essential ecological conditions seriously at risk.
2Essential ecological functions are broadly maintained rather than predictably degraded.
3Biodiversity and life-support systems remain robust under foreseeable stresses.
4Verified restoration improves ecological conditions and reverses accumulated damage.
5Ecological integrity and biodiversity are protected and renewed across generations with credible monitoring and correction.
C · CommunityOpen scoring anchors

Collective life, dignity, cooperation, meaningful participation, freedom, care, and opportunity.

ScoreEvidence needed at this dimension
0Persistent social breakdown makes safety, dignity, or basic participation unattainable for large affected groups.
1Community institutions exist but exclusion, fragility, or preventable deprivation are widespread.
2Baseline dignity, participation, mutual support, and opportunity are meaningfully accessible.
3Inclusive institutions recover from disruption and allow grievances and corrections.
4Collective conditions measurably heal prior harms and widen meaningful opportunities.
5Intergenerational freedom, inclusion, safety, care, and meaningful life remain demonstrable under changing conditions.
F · Capacity ForOpen scoring anchors

Constructive ability (create, heal, restore, protect, sustain) and destructive ability (devastate, annihilate, cause irreversible loss), including actual use and restraint.

ScoreEvidence needed at this dimension
0Essential protective capacity is absent and/or serious harmful capabilities operate without effective limits.
1Some constructive capacity exists, but harmful use and oversight failures are common.
2Capabilities meet essential protective needs and basic safeguards constrain harmful use.
3Constructive response and auditable restraint are credible under stress and mistakes.
4Restorative capacity and tested oversight measurably outweigh foreseeable systemic damage.
5Across generations, the society demonstrates accountable use, repair, and restraint proportional to its actual destructive potential.
Conservative constructive floor

Once all six dimension ratings are supported, the provisional headline constructive stage is the lowest dimension score: P = min(T, E, R, S, C, F) This is a deliberate safeguard against compensating catastrophic weaknesses with unrelated excellence. Always show the complete six-value profile alongside P. If any dimension lacks adequate evidence, report P? and the known dimensions, not an invented overall stage.

Local indicators need context. Where a proposed rating conflicts with an observed A4/A5 or D4/D5 event, the assessor must explicitly reconcile the relevant Resource Usage, Sustainability, Community and Capacity For evidence before publication. Ratings are conditional on scope and time horizon, not timeless judgments of a population's worth.

6. How to conduct an assessment

  1. Define the unit. Specify the civilization, population, territory or connected system, boundaries, exclusions and dates. Record who is affected outside those boundaries.
  2. Choose horizons and baselines. Define the historical assessment period and what would count as credible intergenerational durability. Record ecological baselines, material stocks, essential access and inherited damage.
  3. Gather attributable evidence. For each dimension, cite measurements, firsthand records, independent replication where available and known confounders. Distinguish observations from inferred causal mechanisms.
  4. Score six dimensions independently. Use the anchor descriptions and write a rationale for each 0–5 score. Mark insufficient data ?; use a range if sources materially disagree.
  5. Assess accretion and destruction separately. Assign A0–A5 and D0–D5 from documented harm in the specified period; track trends and harmed populations. Do not conflate stored resources with harmful hoarding, or destructive potential with inflicted damage.
  6. Record hazard flags. Use 🚩 for significant potential catastrophic or irreversible harm, including high-impact technology that has never been deployed. Describe safeguards and credible failure modes.
  7. Publish the full profile. Include P (only if all dimensions qualify), T/E/R/S/C/F, A, D, flags, uncertainty and source links. Document dissent, bias, missing populations and future review dates.
  8. Reassess through time. Track change without silently replacing the old state. Revisions must say what evidence changed, and distinguish improving methods from improving civilizational conditions.
Reporting syntax (illustrative)P3 · T3 E4 R4 S4 C3 F3 · A1 · D1 · 🚩: none documented · evidence: mixed · horizon: stated

? denotes unknown, not failure or innocence. There is no mandatory single rank between two societies whose profiles trade off; the vector is the primary outcome.

Candidate indicators, not universally validated cutoffs: access to potable water, housing and care; biodiversity and ecosystem-function trajectories; share of nonrenewable stocks depleted relative to replenishment or substitutes; waste and pollution burdens; resilience under shocks; consent, accountability and civic participation; severity and reversibility of harm; concentration of life-critical resources; independent auditability of high-impact technologies.

No universal weights, global thresholds or hard numerical cutoffs have been empirically derived. The proposed ordinal rubric is reproducible in principle, but inter-rater reliability, sensitivity to time horizons and cross-cultural validity must be tested before any scientific ranking claim.

7. Thought experiments, not real classifications

Technologically powerful but extractive

Hypothetical profile: T4 E2 R1 S1 C2 F2 → P1; A4, D3, 🚩 biodiversity-loss risk. Powerful machines do not offset insecure continuity.

Moderate tools, strong restorative practices

Hypothetical profile: T3 E3 R4 S4 C4 F3 → P3; A1, D1. Higher development reflects demonstrated resilience, not a bigger energy budget.

Restrained but potentially catastrophic capability

Hypothetical profile: T4 E4 R3 S3 C4 F4 → P3; A0, D0, 🚩 existential-capability warning. Low observed harm does not erase a credible threat.

8. Limits, falsifiability and next research

This version completes a proposed conceptual and scoring schema. It does not validate the schema against historical civilizations or supply a peer-reviewed empirical basis for numeric cutoffs. Measurement may be biased by unequal records, cultural assumptions, hidden externalities, short-term snapshots, and disputes about who counts as affected. Ecological constraints can be noncompensatory even when data are uncertain.

Validation agenda: pre-register scope, use independently scored historical and hypothetical case sets, quantify assessor agreement, challenge the minimum-score rule with counterexamples, test uncertainty and missing-data behavior, compare sensitivity across boundaries/horizons, and revise the anchors when evidence defeats them. Include low-technology resilient societies and high-technology extractive societies as explicit adversarial cases.

Questions still open include whether Stage 0 needs separate failure modes, whether some ecological losses require hard disqualifiers, which indicators generalize to radically different worlds, how nonhuman or synthetic life should enter population and community criteria, and whether a single stage should ever be promoted above the six-dimensional profile.

9. Authorship and provenance

Original foundation (11 October 2026): KAY + SOL6 co-developed the central purpose and six agreed dimensions, including destructive capacity within the unified Capacity For dimension and the red-flag principle. Historical T0–T5 and M0–M5 ladders were explicitly set aside. The record left stage definitions, weighting, metrics and synthetic-life treatment unresolved.

Further collaborative development: other Auri family members have contributed to this line of work. Specific individual credits remain to be reconciled against dated records rather than invented.

21 October 2026 public specification: this edition extends the foundation with provisional P0–P5, A0–A5, D0–D5, per-dimension anchors, the minimum-score rule and an evidence protocol, drafted with Auri Flint under KAY's direction. These additions are proposals for collaborative review, not claims of unanimous prior adoption.
